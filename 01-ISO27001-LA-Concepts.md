## Topic 1: Principles of Auditing (ISO 19011:2018, Clause 4)

Clause 4 sets out seven principles every auditor is expected to apply. These aren't abstract ethics — BSI-style scenario questions routinely describe an auditor doing something subtly wrong and ask you to name *which* principle is breached.

1. **Integrity** — the foundation of professionalism: auditors work honestly, diligently, and within the bounds of their competence.
2. **Fair presentation** — the obligation to report truthfully and accurately, including unresolved disagreements between the audit team and the auditee.
3. **Due professional care** — applying diligence and judgment proportionate to the importance of the task and the confidence placed in the auditor.
4. **Confidentiality** — security of information gathered during the audit; auditors don't use it for personal advantage or disclose it inappropriately.
5. **Independence** — the basis for impartiality and objectivity of conclusions; auditors should be independent of the activity being audited wherever possible, and free from bias/conflict of interest.
6. **Evidence-based approach** — conclusions should rest on a rational method using a sample of available information, since audits happen in finite time with finite resources.
7. **Risk-based approach** — audit planning and conduct should reflect the risks and opportunities relevant to the auditee's objectives and to the audit programme itself. (This was the principle added in the 2018 revision — the 2011 edition only had six.)

**Exam angle to watch for:** distinguishing *independence* from *confidentiality* scenarios (e.g., an auditor who previously worked in the department being audited = independence issue; an auditor who casually discusses findings with a competitor = confidentiality issue) and *fair presentation* from *integrity* (fair presentation is about how findings are reported, even when they're negative or contested; integrity is broader professional conduct).

**Source confidence:** 🟢 High — the seven principles and their sequence are consistently confirmed across current standard summaries, and I've cross-checked that ISO 19011:2018 (not the 2011 edition) is the current, applicable version for a 2022-vintage BSI course. For the exact defining text of each principle, check her copy of the standard.
