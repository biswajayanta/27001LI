## Topic 1: Context of the Organization & ISMS Scope (Clause 4.1–4.4)

This is always the starting point of an implementation, and a common source of exam scenarios because scoping mistakes cascade into everything downstream.

**4.1 — Understanding the organization and its context**
The organization must determine external and internal issues relevant to its purpose that affect its ability to achieve the intended outcome(s) of the ISMS. In practice: regulatory environment, market pressures, technology landscape (external); culture, governance structure, capabilities (internal).

**4.2 — Understanding the needs and expectations of interested parties**
Identify interested parties relevant to the ISMS (customers, regulators, employees, shareholders, suppliers) and determine *which of their requirements will be addressed through the ISMS*. This last part — deciding what's in vs. out — is a 2022-specific clarification and a favourite exam distinction.

**4.3 — Determining the scope of the ISMS**
Scope must be defined considering: the external/internal issues from 4.1, the requirements from 4.2, and interfaces/dependencies between activities performed by the organization and those performed by other organizations. Exclusions from scope are permitted but must be justified — you can't exclude something just because it's inconvenient or risky.

**4.4 — Information security management system**
The organization must establish, implement, maintain, and continually improve the ISMS in accordance with the standard's requirements. This is the "umbrella" clause tying 4.1–4.3 to the rest of the standard.

**Exam angle to watch for:** scenarios where a company tries to exclude a business unit, subsidiary, or outsourced function from scope for convenience (cost, politics, difficulty) rather than because it's genuinely outside the interfaces/dependencies. The correct answer is almost always "the exclusion needs a risk-based justification, not a business-convenience justification."
