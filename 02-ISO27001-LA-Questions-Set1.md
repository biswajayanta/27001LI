# ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Lead Auditor — Questions, Set 1
### Topic 1: Principles of Auditing (ISO 19011:2018, Clause 4)

Attempt all questions before checking the answers file. Name the specific principle, don't just say "that's wrong" — the exam wants precision.

---

**Q1.** An external auditor conducting a certification audit was, until 14 months ago, employed as an IT manager at the organization being audited, in a role with direct responsibility for the very access control processes she is now assigned to audit. She believes she can still be objective. Which principle is most directly at risk, and why does her own belief in her objectivity not resolve the concern?

---

**Q2.** During an audit, the audit team finds strong evidence of a serious nonconformity in supplier security management. The auditee's management strongly disputes the finding and asks the lead auditor to "soften" the wording in the report to avoid it looking as bad, in exchange for smoother cooperation on the rest of the audit. What principle obliges the lead auditor to resist this, and what should the report reflect if the disagreement can't be resolved?

---

**Q3.** An auditor, short on time near the end of a long conducting-the-audit day, decides to conclude that access reviews are being performed properly based on a single verbal assurance from an IT manager, without asking to see any review records or sampling any actual accounts. Which principle is compromised, and what should have happened instead?

---

**Q4.** After an audit, one team member mentions details of the client's unreleased product security roadmap (learned incidentally during the audit) to a friend at a competing firm, describing it as "not a big deal since it's not in the official report." Which principle has been breached, and is the auditor's justification valid?

---

**Q5. (Multiple choice)** A lead auditor, under significant time pressure, reviews only 2 of the 40 relevant records available for a control area and generalizes a conclusion of "fully effective" across the whole population, without any stated rationale for why that sample size was sufficient. This is most directly a failure of which principle?

A. Confidentiality — because record access wasn't properly authorized
B. Independence — because the auditor was rushed by management
C. Evidence-based approach — because conclusions should rest on a rational method applied to an appropriately justified sample, not an arbitrary shortcut
D. Fair presentation — because the finding wasn't communicated to the auditee
